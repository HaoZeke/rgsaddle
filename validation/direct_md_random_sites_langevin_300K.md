# Random-vacancy survival, Mishin Cu

The vacancy is the lattice site nearest the cell center. The <100> dumbbell host is drawn uniformly from the other sites of the 13³ cell, and the dumbbell axis uniformly from the three orientations. Under periodic boundaries the separation is uniform over the cell, which is the ensemble in section 2 of Mwungeli, Liu, and Demkowicz, Phys. Rev. Materials 9, 023601 (2025). The fit is ln S(t) = -(k/V_model) t through the origin, sampled every 25 ps, with V_model the cell volume and S the Kaplan-Meier survival, in which an uncaptured trajectory is censored at the end of its window; with equal windows S = n(t)/N. Samples after S reaches zero are left out. V_iv/Ω = 4 α k / (a D_i) with α = 2/3 and Ω = a³/4, from K_iv = V_iv D_i / (α a²) = V_iv ν in section 1 of that paper, where ν is the rate of one of the eight translation jumps of the dumbbell. That ν is the defect's own jump rate, so the D_i the relation needs is (2/3)a²ν, the diffusivity of the defect when its successive jumps are uncorrelated; `direct_md_dynamics.md` tests that, and also converts k with ν counted from site changes.

Table 1 of that paper converts its k with the D_i in its own Table 1 column, which it quotes from Ref. [12], B. Zhang et al., Phys. Rev. Materials 6, 053605 (2022). Ref. [12] took the mean-square displacement of every atom, its Eq. (5), and its erratum, Phys. Rev. Materials 9, 069901(E) (2025), multiplies that per-atom diffusivity by the 4951 atoms of its cell. The product is the tracer sum N D* = Σ_i ⟨|Δr_i|²⟩ / 6t, which is the defect diffusivity times the tracer correlation factor (`direct_md_dynamics.md`). The paper's own trajectories are NPH for 30 ps, then NVE with velocity rescaling, at its lattice constant. Our k comes from the Langevin dynamics in the CSV. The N D* column converts our k with the tracer sum measured on free-dumbbell trajectories with the same dynamics, potential, and cell (`direct_md_sia_tracer_p0_gamma0.1_dt10fs.md`), the convention of Table 1. The defect-D column uses the Wigner-Seitz defect diffusivity of those dynamics (`direct_md_sia_d_p0.md`). σ_k is the standard deviation over 400 bootstrap resamples of the trajectories. The bootstrap σ column carries only that; the total σ columns add the relative error of the D_i used in quadrature.

A trajectory is captured when every Wigner-Seitz site of the ideal lattice holds one atom at 4 consecutive samples, 1.00 ps; the capture time is the first of them. Sites move with the cell and the centroid drift is removed. The last two outcome columns count sites holding other than one atom at the start and at the end.

At 300 K, Table 1 D_i = 0.143 ± 0.006 Å²/ps; measured N D* = 0.139 ± 0.004 Å²/ps; measured defect D = 0.272 ± 0.016 Å²/ps.

At the longest window, 1500 ps, k = 43.01 ± 6.70 Å³/ps. With the Table 1 D_i, V_iv = 220.83 ± 34.39 Ω (bootstrap) or ± 35.62 Ω (with D_i). With the measured N D*, V_iv = 226.90 ± 35.99 Ω. With the measured defect D, V_iv = 116.22 ± 19.31 Ω.
The constant-hazard maximum likelihood over the same 1500 ps, with every uncaptured trajectory censored at the end of its window, is k = 46.41 Å³/ps from 47 captures in 96 trajectories, 95 % interval 34.87 to 61.77 from ln k ± 1.96/√captures. V_iv is 238.30 Ω (95 % 179.04 to 317.16) with the Table 1 D_i, 244.85 Ω (95 % 183.96 to 325.88) with the measured N D*, and 125.42 Ω (95 % 94.23 to 166.92) with the measured defect D; these intervals leave out the D_i error.
